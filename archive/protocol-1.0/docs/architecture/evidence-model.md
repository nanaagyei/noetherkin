# Evidence and quality

An evidence record makes exactly one competency claim in one project context. `task_id` may be null for a baseline observation; task-related records must reference their task. `fact` records what was observed, when, and supporting artifacts. `interpretation` separately records independence, signal direction, target level, strength and rationale. A successful test does not automatically prove the interpretation. Author and verification reviewer are attributable registered principals.

Verification statuses are `unverified` (not corroborated), `verified` (artifact inspected and observation corroborated), and `refuted` (artifact contradicts observation). A failed test can be verified evidence: verification concerns truth of the observation, not task success. Corrections supersede the record; never erase a contrary signal. Reviewer cannot be the learner whose behavior is evaluated. Team lead verifies formal evidence; peers may publish provisional unverified evidence but cannot certify it for formal evaluation.

Strong evidence requires inspected reproducible artifacts, a direct observation, meaningful stated scope, known assistance and an explicit connection to the competency. Moderate evidence has corroboration but narrower context; weak evidence is limited, indirect or uncertain. `strong` cannot accompany unverified/refuted verification or unknown assistance. Missing artifacts must be recorded as a gap, not substituted with a fabricated URI. Unknown assistance uses `known: false`, null highest level and no inferred independence; known assistance has integer highest level equal to the maximum event level, or 0 if independently observed work has no assistance events.

Task evidence includes the assistance events relevant to that competency and observation interval from the canonical task history. This is an immutable observation snapshot, not a second editable assistance ledger. Later-discovered assistance requires correction and reassessment of affected judgments. Each event names who provided help and what behavior it affected. Separate evidence can describe learner comprehension after someone else implemented a change.

A formal assessment/review may cite unverified or refuted records to explain uncertainty or contrary signals, but affirmative capability claims must use verified supporting evidence. A promotion cannot be supported solely by weak, unknown-assistance or self-reported material. Evaluate repetition, recency relative to technology/context change, diversity of independent situations, scope, independence and contrary evidence qualitatively. Repeated citations to the same artifact are not repeated demonstrations. Simulated incidents never establish real production operations experience. Fixture evidence is excluded from live judgments.

Published evidence cannot be deleted to improve a learner's history. Missing, inaccessible or changed artifact revisions make dependent judgments stale and require explicit review. This protocol provides provenance and review discipline, not cryptographic proof of learner authorship or an external credential.
