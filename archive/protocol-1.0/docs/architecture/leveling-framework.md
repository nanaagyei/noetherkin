# Engineering levels

The exact behavioral expectations for all thirteen dimensions and six levels are in `catalog/levels.yaml`. These are internal simulator levels with no implied equivalence to employers, job titles, hiring assessments or credentials.

| Level | Scope anchor |
| --- | --- |
| E0 Apprentice | Guided bounded steps with explanations |
| E1 Junior Engineer | Independently verified localized changes |
| E2 Independent Engineer | Unfamiliar cross-component work |
| E3 Product Engineer | Product/service outcomes through operation |
| E4 Senior / Systems Engineer | Ambiguous systemic work and risk reduction |
| E5 Lead Engineer | Sustained practices and decisions across teams |

Expectations are observable behaviors rather than time served. They apply in the selected specialization and all core competencies. Production and collaboration behavior may be assessed in labeled simulation contexts, but cannot be represented as real employment or production experience. E5 simulated coordination must include attributable interactions and resulting artifacts; merely writing a leadership plan does not demonstrate its effects.

Promotion considers every next-level dimension explicitly and relates its cited evidence to core and selected specialization competencies. A narrow strength cannot substitute for missing ownership, testing or communication. More assistance changes the claim that can be made; independent work in an unrelated easy task cannot establish next-level scope. Effective level is derived from promotion decisions, never an average of competency levels. Only adjacent promotions are allowed; E5 is terminal for V1.

Use the [review protocol](review-model.md) for decision authority and outcome semantics. Task count, elapsed time, course completion, cloning and subjective encouragement cannot grant promotion.
